# Support this work

These public RPC endpoints are being stood up as one of the public goods kept in the
[`ethereumclassic`](https://github.com/ethereumclassic) GitHub organization, alongside
the [Core-Geth](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/core-geth) client, the network's
bootnodes and the
[peer discovery lists](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/discv4-dns-lists), so that
they belong to the network rather than to whoever is paying for them that year. This
page says who maintains and funds them, why they need sponsors, and how a sponsor
is credited. It is the one place this repository publishes its funding routes. If
you find them anywhere else, check them against this page first.

## Who maintains them

These public goods are maintained in the `ethereumclassic` GitHub organization by
Ethereum Classic's core developers and open community contributions, who do the work,
together with the [Ethereum Classic DAO](https://ethereumclassicdao.org), the standing
legal entity that maintains and funds them as the funder of last resort and the floor
under the work when nobody else is paying.

**The work happens in public.** Pull requests are open for anyone to read, and every
repository in the `ethereumclassic` organization carries more than one admin and more
than one maintainer, so no single person or employer is a point of failure. The
repositories stay there no matter who maintains or funds them.

**The Ethereum Classic DAO is a Wyoming DAO LLC**, and what gives the network a legal
counterpart when the world asks for one: funding has to be received and reported in a
tax jurisdiction, and an organization sponsoring the work has to contract with someone.
It does not control or lead Ethereum Classic, and it is not the only organization that
can fund this work.

Moving Ethereum Classic's public goods into the `ethereumclassic` GitHub organization
began in 2024, through the ETC Cooperative's own efforts: its published roadmap of July
2024 proposed moving Core-Geth into that organization, and `ethereumclassic/core-geth`
was created in December 2024. Before that move, the work passed from one organization
to the next as each closed or left: ETCDEV in 2018, ETC Labs in 2021, and the ETC
Cooperative, which entered maintenance mode at the end of 2024 and is due to dissolve.
Along the way the client changed repositories, the bootnodes and the peer discovery
lists changed operators, the `v1.12` line went 21 months without security maintenance
in `etclabscore/core-geth`, and the public endpoint `etc.rivet.link` went offline when
its contract ended. What that cost the client is published in full, release by release
and audit by audit: [the record is on its security page](https://coregeth.com/security/).

With the ETC Cooperative's dissolution, Ethereum Classic stakeholders such as
mining pools, exchanges and service providers should use
<security@ethereumclassic.net> as their point of contact. A person answers it:
one of the core developers who maintain this work. Sponsorship has its own
address, given below.

**Sponsors are credited here.** A sponsoring organization is added to this table
with its name, a link and the years it has contributed, and can be credited for
the specific work it funds. A sponsor that prefers not to be named is not listed.

## Why sponsor public goods

If you are a stakeholder of Ethereum Classic, you rely on this infrastructure.
Anyone adding the network to a wallet, trying it out or starting to build on it
needs a public endpoint that answers, before or without a node of their own.
**Ethereum Classic has no protocol-layer funding mechanism for its critical
infrastructure or its core developers**, so no part of the network's fee or
issuance revenue pays for this work.

This is not hypothetical. Three times the organization paying for the work has left.
The last time, the `v1.12` line went 21 months without security maintenance, one of the
flaws left open was used against Core-Geth `v1.12` bootnodes, and `etc.rivet.link` went
offline when its contract ended. Keeping the work in the `ethereumclassic` organization
is meant to stop the next handover costing the same.

Ethereum Classic's core developers do the day-to-day work, and the Ethereum Classic DAO
is the standing legal entity that maintains and funds it as the funder of last resort.
A last resort is a floor rather than a plan: what the work needs is steady funding from
the stakeholders who depend on it, so that it no longer rests on a single backer.

## How to sponsor

**An invoiced agreement, for organizations.** If your business depends on this
infrastructure, a maintenance sponsorship can be invoiced against a purchase
order like any other vendor relationship, which is usually what a finance
department needs. Write to <donations@ethereumclassic.net> with what you run and
the period you want to cover.

**A direct transfer.** For anyone who prefers to send funds directly, an address
is provided on request. Write to <donations@ethereumclassic.net>. If a large
transfer is planned, send a small one first and confirm receipt at the same
address.

**This project does not publish a donation address.** An address shown in a
screenshot, a fork, a mirror or a message did not come from this project unless
it arrived in a reply from <donations@ethereumclassic.net>.

Funding mail goes to `ethereumclassic.net`, the domain for the public goods
maintained in the `ethereumclassic` GitHub organization.

## What sponsorship pays for

Keeping these endpoints fit to serve a live network: standing up and running the
gateway and the nodes behind it, keeping the domain registered and its DNS served, and
keeping the configuration and the admission list current.
[Core-Geth's support page](https://docs.coregeth.com/support/) describes what it
pays for in the client, and
[the discovery lists' support page](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/discv4-dns-lists/blob/main/docs/support.md)
what it pays for in those lists.

## What sponsorship does not buy

**No place in the admission list, and no better service.** An endpoint is admitted
on the same criteria as every other, whoever runs it and whoever sponsors this
work, and the admission list is committed here as endpoints are admitted, where
anyone can check it. A sponsor gets the same endpoints and the same limits as
everyone else. Being credited records a contribution; it is not an endorsement of the
sponsor's products or services.

Requests and reports are welcome from anyone, sponsor or not, in
[this repository's issues](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/public-rpc/issues).
